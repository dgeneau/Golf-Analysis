Your bag

Every club on one yardage axis — box is the middle half of your shots, whisker the full range, tick the median. Gaps and overlaps in the set read straight off it.

Held-out shots

A shot's distance is measured to wherever you played the next shot from, so a swing the sensor missed makes one segment cover two strokes — a drive plus a wedge becomes one enormous drive. These are kept out of your bag but never deleted. If one of them was real, put it back and it returns to your numbers straight away. Only the long end is ever questioned: a missed swing can only make a distance too big, and short shots are real golf.

Sessions

Data shown is a synthetic session generated by the Downrange simulator (including sensor-range clipping and ball-strike shock) and run through the identical detection and metrics pipeline that live Bluetooth data uses. ⚠ marks swings where the ±16 g accelerometer saturated — hand speed is a floor and path is suppressed from coaching on those swings. Path carries about ±15° per swing at the 60 Hz stream rate, which is why it is reported as a session tendency rather than a per-swing number; attack angle is noisier still and is not reported at all.

Feedback on every swing

Downrange reads your swing from one small sensor on your wrist — your tempo, club path and speed, plus one thing to work on after each shot.

Wear it on your lead wrist

Strap the sensor to your lead wrist (left, for right-handed golfers), flat on top of the wrist. Keep the strap snug so it can't rotate — that's what keeps your numbers honest.

Strike channel

The microphone hears the ball 800× more precisely than the wrist sensor can feel it. With it on, impact is timed from the strike itself.

Lay the phone on the ground about half a metre behind the ball, screen up, clear of your swing and the divot. Close is what separates your ball from the next bay along: at half a metre yours lands about 9 dB louder than their driver, at a metre and a half theirs wins.
